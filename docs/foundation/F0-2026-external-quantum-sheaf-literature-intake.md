## 1. Why this pass is different from another synthetic easy example

An independently published mathematical object is **necessary, not sufficient**, for a genuinely independent cryptographic inverse assumption. Before treating a paper as a MORPH source, require its **exact original computational target**, published public data and verifier, unconditional polynomial honest generator and distribution, polynomial witness-size bound, accepted equivalence fibre (any witness, not planted secret), and applicability of *both* known classical and quantum algorithms. A paper's NP-hardness theorem or quantum/classical separation may concern a **different** promise task or worst-case distribution from the proposed honest inversion experiment.

This file screens externally documented objects; it **does not** supply a source-faithful new MORPH inverse relation, replace the [15-field admission gate](../97-morph-r0-cross-line-postmortem.md#11-re-entry-gate-for-any-new-morph-family), or claim independent peer review of the screened papers.

## 2. March/April 2026: nonlinear sheaf morphism encryption, strict version provenance

**Primary version evidence:** Lucas Alaniz Pintos, [*The Alaniz Cipher: Encryption from Nonlinear Sheaf Morphisms over Graphs*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401670518_The_Alaniz_Cipher_Encryption_from_Nonlinear_Sheaf_Morphisms_over_Graphs), March 2026, DOI [10.5281/zenodo.18908260](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18908260). A later author preprint, [*The Alaniz Cipher v3: Sheaf-Based Post-Quantum Encryption via Vector-Valued Finite Field Permutations*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404175708_The_Alaniz_Cipher_v3_Sheaf-Based_Post-Quantum_Encryption_via_Vector-Valued_Finite_Field_Permutations), April 2026, DOI [10.5281/zenodo.19746033](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19746033), makes two *distinct* statements:

- It **self-reports** that its prior versions [1,2] were broken by a chosen-plaintext interpolation and Gröbner-basis attack.
- It **claims** that changed vector-valued permutations and key restrictions repair this issue and establish IND-CPA security, with a Fujisaki–Okamoto path to an IND-CCA KEM. These are **author claims from a preprint**, not independently reproduced breaks, a completed MORPH original-verifier reduction, or an external endorsement of v3 security.

**Source-first admission screen:** the earlier abstract claims NP-hardness via reduction from mature Multivariate Quadratic (MQ) systems and discusses private local coefficients in a cellular-sheaf encryption. For MORPH, before even formulating a hypothesis, one must extract the actual **public encryption/evaluation data** and full **public witness verifier** for a precise inversion problem, and describe the unconditional key/message source. A worst-case reduction *to the inverse problem* from MQ does **not** by itself make the honestly generated ciphertext distribution average-case hard, and a new geometric encoding or private coefficients do not establish mathematically independent Field 15. An independent crypto review must also respect later version replacements and the author's self-reported failures rather than citing the oldest abstract as if it were a surviving security result.

**Disposition: EXTERNAL HOLD / NOT ADMITTED.** Neither version is nominated or declared secure. The v1/v2 breaks are **reported by the v3 author**, not validated anew here. No claim is made that v3 is broken, that a public-key interface is impossible, or that its detailed security proofs have been independently audited.

## 3. June 2026: a topological problem made efficient for quantum computation

**Peer-reviewed theorem:** Casper Gyurik, Alexander Schmidhuber, Robbie King, Vedran Dunjko and Ryu Hayakawa, [*Provable Quantum Speedups for Computing Persistence in Topological Data Analysis*](https://doi.org/10.1103/gvys-hl8h), *PRX Quantum* **7**, 020361 (16 June 2026). For the authors' specifically formulated **Harmonic Persistence** promise problem, with the required representation of a harmonic representative as part of the input, the paper proves \(\mathsf{BQP}_1\)-hardness and **membership in \(\mathsf{BQP}\)**. It is evidence for a **quantum advantage** over classical computation for this promise task, **not** for computational hardness against quantum attackers.

**Direct cryptographic exclusion, with careful scope:** proposing **the same decision/promise problem on the same efficiently accessible input representation** as a post-quantum hard-to-decide relation contradicts the published efficient quantum algorithm. It does **not** imply that every persistent-homology calculation, any-witness *search* analogue, succinct-source variant, or a separate trapdoor relation is quantum-easy. No source-faithful MORPH OWF could be inferred from a decision-problem complexity result in either direction.

**Disposition:** direct *same-promise decision-hardness* interpretation **REJECT** for a post-quantum foundation. Any altered polynomial honest-source *search* relation is **UNDEFINED / HOLD** until the full public source, original verifier, classical/quantum comparison and positive independent Field 15 are supplied. Do not conflate high-dimensional topological structure, worst-case classical difficulty or \(\mathsf{BQP}\)-hardness with resistance to quantum algorithms.

Neither worst-case shortest-word hardness nor good coding-theory distance/testability suffices for a new MORPH assumption.
